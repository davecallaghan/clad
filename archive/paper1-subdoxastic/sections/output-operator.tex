\section{The Output Operator and Its Axiom Failures}
\label{sec:output-operator}

\subsection{Defining the operator}
\label{sec:defining-o}

Fix a model $M$: a function from input strings to distributions over output
strings, together with a decoding procedure. Fix a \emph{prompt-form} $\pi$: an
equivalence class of input contexts presenting a query in a given surface
formulation---wording, format, system prompt, few-shot exemplars, position in
context. Fix an \emph{assertion threshold} $t \in (0,1)$: a level of output
probability above which we count the model as committing to a content rather
than merely mentioning it.

\begin{definition}[Output operator]
\label{def:output}
$\Out_M \phi$ holds iff model $M$, under prompt-form $\pi$, produces an
assertion with content $\phi$ at output probability at least $t$.
\end{definition}

Four comments. First, $\Out_M$ is officially three-place, $\Out_M(\phi, \pi, t)$;
I suppress the parameters where they are held fixed, and it is precisely the
ineliminability of the $\pi$-parameter that drives the hyperintensionality
result. For a doxastic system, relativisation to $\pi$ would be a curable
indexicality: the system believes what it believes, and the prompt merely
elicits it. My claim will be that for $M$ there is no $\pi$-invariant state
\emph{governing} assertion for the prompt to elicit. That is weaker than the
claim that no $\pi$-invariant state exists at all, and
Section~\ref{sec:internal} explains why the weaker claim is the one I need and
the one the evidence supports.

Second, ``content $\phi$'' presupposes a mapping from output strings to
propositions. I assume a charitable, contextually standard interpretation
function---a substantive assumption, and one under independent scrutiny
\citep{mandelkern2024}---since the argument is that $\Out_M$ misbehaves even granting the
friendliest content-assignment available.

Third, the threshold is defined over \emph{output probability}, not credence.
This matters more than it may appear. To speak of the model's credence would be
to ascribe a graded doxastic state in the very definition of the operator whose
non-doxastic character is at issue. Output probability is a behavioural quantity,
measurable from the decoding distribution, and commits us to nothing.

Fourth, the threshold idealises away sampling noise. The failures below are
failures at high output probability, not artefacts of temperature.

One methodological point before proceeding, because it determines how much each
of the following subsections is worth. Some failures can be demonstrated with
$\pi$ held fixed---within a single context, with all premises explicit---and
some can be demonstrated only by varying $\pi$. The first kind is independent
evidence. The second is the RE-failure of Section~\ref{sec:re-failure} appearing
under another name, and I mark it as such rather than counting it twice.

\subsection{Failure of K}
\label{sec:k-failure}

K licenses detachment inside the operator: from $\Out_M(\phi \rightarrow \psi)$
and $\Out_M \phi$, infer $\Out_M \psi$. The failure is among the
best-documented behavioural signatures of these systems. A model asserts, under
direct query, a general conditional---a rule of a game, a regulatory principle,
an arithmetic identity schema; asserts, under direct query, an instance of its
antecedent; and then denies or fails to produce the consequent, including within
a single context window where both premises are tokens it has itself just
generated.

The reversal-curse results provide a clean instance: models trained on ``$A$ is
$B$'' reliably assert it, yet fail to assert ``$B$ is $A$,'' even where the
bridging equivalence is separately available \citep{berglund2024}. The diagnosis
is architectural. Assertion probability is computed by forward passes over
surface contexts, and there is no mechanism whose function is to close the
asserted set under modus ponens. Detachment occurs when the training
distribution happens to contain the closure, and not otherwise.

This failure is demonstrable with $\pi$ fixed, and so counts as independent
evidence.

\subsection{Failure of M}
\label{sec:m-failure}

M says that asserting a conjunction commits one to each conjunct. $\Out_M$ fails
it: a model asserts a conjunctive content fluently, because the conjunction is
distributionally natural as a surface string, while denying one conjunct under
isolated query. Biographical and regulatory summaries are the common case---the
model asserts that a compound was approved for an indication \emph{and} carries a
particular contraindication, then denies the contraindication when asked about it
directly. The conjunction was licensed by the statistics of summary-genre text,
not by conjunction introduction over independently held commitments.

Two caveats, in the interest of not overselling this. First, a reader may object
that M should hold automatically for a probability threshold, since a marginal
cannot fall below a joint. The reply is that the two assertions are elicited
under different prompt-forms, so no single distribution is being
marginalised---which is exactly the point, and shows again that the
$\pi$-parameter is doing real work. Second, and for the same reason, this is not
independent evidence: the demonstration varies $\pi$, and so belongs to the
RE-failure family. I include it because M is validated by every monotonic logic,
so its failure locates $\Out_M$ below the regular systems before we reach RE.

\subsection{Failure of D}
\label{sec:d-failure}

D forbids asserting both $\phi$ and $\neg\phi$. $\Out_M$ violates it flagrantly
across prompt-forms: sycophancy results show models asserting $\phi$ under
neutral framing and $\neg\phi$ when the prompt signals a user preference for
$\neg\phi$, both above threshold \citep{sharma2024}. But D also fails with $\pi$
fixed. Models produce, in one continuous generation, sentences that contradict
each other under any reasonable content-assignment, each locally licensed by its
immediate context \citep{mundler2024}; and measured consistency under
paraphrase is well below what a consistency-maintaining process would deliver
\citep{elazar2021}.

The point is not that a doxastic system cannot harbour contradictions. It is
that for KD45 belief, contradiction is a malfunction relative to a
consistency-enforcing competence. For $\Out_M$ there is nothing whose function is
consistency for the contradiction to be a malfunction of. This failure is
demonstrable with $\pi$ fixed.

\subsection{Failure of 4 and 5}
\label{sec:introspection-failure}

The introspection axioms presuppose that the system has access to its own
attitude-states. $\Out_M \phi \rightarrow \Out_M \Out_M \phi$ would require that
when $M$ asserts $\phi$, it also asserts, above threshold, that it asserts
$\phi$---that its self-reports track its first-order dispositions. They do not,
reliably. Self-reports are generated by the same next-token machinery as
everything else, trained on human self-report text, and correlate imperfectly
with actual output distributions. A model asserts that it knows nothing about a
topic and, several turns later, asserts detailed contents about it; or claims
certainty while its distribution over the relevant completions is close to
uniform.

Two qualifications are owed here, because the evidence is genuinely mixed and I
do not want the argument to rest on a caricature. Models do exhibit non-trivial
ability to evaluate their own outputs \citep{kadavath2022}, and can be trained
to express calibrated uncertainty in words \citep{lin2022}. What that work
establishes is aggregate calibration on a benchmark distribution. What axiom 5
demands is something else: reliable recognition of the system's own epistemic
boundary, claim by claim---formally, that
\[
  \forall \phi\, \forall c \;\bigl(\neg K_A(\phi \mid c) \rightarrow
  K_A\bigl(\neg K_A(\phi \mid c)\bigr)\bigr).
\]
Aggregate calibration and per-claim boundary recognition are different
properties, and only the second supports abstention in a consequential setting.
It is not enough that a system sometimes expresses uncertainty; the requirement
is that it reliably identify what it does not know. Negative introspection fails
a fortiori, since the absence of an assertion disposition is nowhere represented
in a form the model can report.

I note in passing that 4 and 5 sit awkwardly on an output operator at all.
``$M$ asserts that $M$ asserts $\phi$'' iterates an operator whose operand
should be a proposition, over a fact about $M$. The awkwardness is itself mild
evidence for the thesis: these axioms were designed for attitude operators with
introspective access, and their ill-fit is a symptom of the category difference
I am arguing for.

\subsection{Failure of RE}
\label{sec:re-failure}

I have saved the critical case. RE is the weakest closure condition in the
classical hierarchy: it demands only that logically equivalent contents receive
the same treatment. \emph{Any} operator over propositions---any operator whose
objects are individuated no more finely than sets of possible worlds---validates
RE automatically. RE-failure therefore diagnoses something categorical: the
operator's objects are not propositions but finer-grained entities.

$\Out_M$ fails RE, robustly. Let $\phi$ and $\psi$ be logically equivalent
formulations: contraposed conditionals; ``no $A$ are $B$'' against ``all $A$ are
non-$B$''; a claim and its double negation; a quantity in words against digits;
active against passive renderings of the same relational claim. The record on
paraphrase and format sensitivity is substantial: performance and assertion
behaviour diverge sharply across semantically equivalent formattings
\citep{sclar2024}, and models perform comparably under prompts whose content is
irrelevant or misleading, which decouples response from propositional content
\citep{webson2022}. The reversal-curse case of Section~\ref{sec:k-failure} is
the cleanest instance, since for identity statements ``$A$ is $B$'' and ``$B$ is
$A$'' are equivalent and the asymmetry is systematic rather than noisy.

Why is this constitutive rather than noise? Because the quantity the
architecture computes---conditional probability of token sequences given token
sequences---is defined over strings, and strings individuate more finely than
logical equivalence: equivalent contents are in general distinct strings with
distinct distributional histories. There is no representation of the proposition
``behind'' the formulations to which sameness of treatment could be pegged.
Insofar as equivalent formulations are treated alike, that is a contingent
achievement of training coverage, degrading predictably as formulations become
distributionally rarer.

The distinction I need is between an empirical tendency and a validity.
RE-conformity for $\Out_M$ is a tendency with principled exceptions; RE-validity
requires exceptionlessness in principle. A critic will observe that human
RE-conformity is also a tendency with principled exceptions, and that observation
is correct and important; I take it up in Section~\ref{sec:human-parallel},
which is where this argument is won or lost.

The pattern is not a scattering of independent defects. It has a single root:
$\Out_M$ is computed over surface formulations by processes with no access to,
and no function of tracking, propositional identity or logical relations among
contents. The axiom failures are symptoms. RE-failure names the disease.
